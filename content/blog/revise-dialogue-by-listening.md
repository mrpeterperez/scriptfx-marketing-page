Listening is useful because it takes away your ability to skim. A reader has to move through every exchange, including the lines your eyes have learned to skip. That can reveal where a scene is working harder than it needs to.

## Listen for a change

A conversation becomes a scene when something changes. Before the read, write down what each character wants and what becomes different by the end. Listen for the point where that change actually happens.

If the scene ends in the same emotional and practical position where it began, ask whether the exchange needs a sharper turn, a different ending, or a place inside another scene.

## Separate repetition from pressure

Repeated words are not automatically a problem. Repetition can show panic, evasion, or an attempt to regain control. The question is whether the repeated line changes the pressure.

Consider this short example:

```text
MARA
You said the door would be open.

ELI
It was.

MARA
Then why do you have the key?
```

The last line shifts the argument from the door to Eli. Another explanation about the locked door would need to earn its place after that turn.

## Check what the audience already knows

During a listening pass, mark information you have already received through action or another line. Ask whether the character has a reason to say it again. A character can repeat information strategically, but the repetition should reveal something about the exchange.

Try removing one explanatory line and hearing the scene again. If the intention remains clear, the shorter version may create more room for performance.

## Distinguish writing from delivery

A hesitant read can make a decisive character sound uncertain. A rushed performance can hide a useful beat. Before rewriting a line, try a different interpretation or read it yourself.

Generated voices are a listening aid, not a final judgment on the writing. Compare what the words support with what a particular take happened to deliver.

## Make one deliberate pass

Choose a single purpose for the rewrite: sharpen the objective, shorten an explanation, or give the scene a clearer turn. Then read the whole scene again. Small changes can affect the balance of an exchange more than you expect.
